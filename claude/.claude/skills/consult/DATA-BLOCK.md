# The data behind a position — the block a brief carries when numbers do

Reached from `consult/SKILL.md` step 2 and `review/SKILL.md` step 2. One home
for the rule; each brief template carries the section's slot and the output
item that consumes it.

## When it fires

The position, the cause, or the change under review rests on a count, a rate,
a timing or any measurement this session produced — a production database
query, a log-analytics run, a query over session history, a project's own
evidence command, an eval or benchmark result, a hand classification of rows. A number quoted from a doc someone else measured
does not fire it; that number is a premise, checked at its source like any
other. When nothing in the brief rests on session-produced data, the section
is deleted and the output item with it.

## Why the method goes on trial before the conclusions

A voice that starts from the conclusions critiques the numbers inside their
frame, and the defects that decide a round are upstream of any conclusion: a
sweep that ran over every turn when the instruction governs a window, a
predicate that measures the reviewer's vocabulary rather than the outcome, a
classification one rater made with a soft boundary, a denominator taken from
the wrong population, a receipt that reads differently at its source. Those
are only findable when the voice holds the query and the raw output, so the
block hands them over rather than summarising them.

## The section, as the brief carries it

```markdown
## The data behind this position

«Observed only — what was pulled, how, and what came back; every conclusion
drawn from it belongs to the position below, never here.»

- **Source and window:** «the system queried and the dates it covers; the
  provider or environment where that matters»
- **Population and denominator:** «what one row is; how many; what was
  excluded and why (self, continuations, sidechains, another provider)»
- **Predicates and queries:** «by absolute path — the query files and their
  saved outputs in the scratchpad; a predicate stated inline when it is one
  line; and what the voice can run: the command and the credential it holds,
  or that it judges from the saved queries and outputs alone»
- **Classification:** «how rows became categories — regex, a hand read, a
  model — by how many raters, and where the boundary was soft»
- **Coverage gaps:** «what the method cannot see: stored text truncated at a
  limit, a signature the sweep would miss, dates outside the window, rows the
  index does not hold»
- **Not measured:** «the number the argument would like to have and does not»
- **Receipts:** «the two or three rows the position leans on hardest, by id,
  so the voice reads them at the source before reading the position»
```

## The output item the voice fills first

```markdown
0. **The method** — before any conclusion: is the right data pulled for the
   question, is data missing that would change the answer, is the analysis
   approach sound, and which of the position's counts or claims the method
   cannot support as stated. Where you doubt a count, re-run or vary the
   predicate if the brief says you can, and otherwise say what you would
   run and what would change if it came back different. "Sound, with these
   limits" is a real answer.
```

The item sits before the verdict the round exists for, so a conclusion is
judged only after the ground it stands on: item 0 in a consult and a full
review, and in a goal review the item after the expectations written blind.
The template's heading wins over the one shown above — a review says "this
change" where a consult says "this position". In a diagnosis brief
the block's bullets go inside "What we observe" without the heading, since
that section already is the observed register.

## What the author does before dispatch

Save every query and its raw output beside the brief and cite them by path —
a voice cannot judge a predicate it cannot read. Results that exist only as
tool output in the conversation are re-run into files: displayed output is
truncated, and a transcription is not raw. Write the block in the
observed register: a number, where it came from, what it excludes. State the
window the instruction under discussion governs and confirm the sweep ran over
that window and not the whole session or project. Name the rater and the
boundary: "one reader, 42 texts, hybrid-versus-countershape was a judgment
call" is what lets the voice re-read three of them and disagree.
